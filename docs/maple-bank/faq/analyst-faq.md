# Card Disputes Analyst FAQ

## Which policy version applies when the policy changes?

The version in force on the **transaction date**, not the submission date. A charge dated October 20, 2026 uses version 3.0 (90 days) even if the dispute arrives in November. See Card Dispute Policy 3.1.

## How do I count the days for the time limit?

From the transaction's **posting date** to the date the customer **submitted** the dispute. Use the rules tool; don't count by hand.

## What if the merchant already refunded the customer?

Then nothing is owed: deny under policy 4.4 and tell the customer which refund posted, and when.

## The customer says they were overcharged, but the receipt is in US dollars.

Check whether the posted CAD amount equals the US amount converted at the network rate plus our 2.5% conversion fee. If it does, it isn't an error: deny under policy 4.3 and explain the conversion.

## The customer doesn't recognise a charge, but it looks like their grocery store.

Statement descriptors are often shortened or carry a payment processor's prefix such as `SQ *`. If the customer has a history of purchases with that merchant, it's genuine: deny under policy 4.1 and explain the descriptor. If you see signs of fraud instead, send the case to the fraud team.

## Can I credit a not-received order before the delivery date?

No. If the expected delivery date hadn't passed when the customer submitted, deny for now (policy 4.6) and tell them they can submit again after that date.

## The receipt photo is blurry.

Don't guess the amount. Request a clearer copy (policy 3.4).

## Who approves a credit of exactly $1,000.00?

An analyst. Only credits of **more than** $1,000.00 need a senior analyst (policy 3.3).

## The customer cancelled a subscription but can't remember the date.

Ask for the cancellation confirmation (policy 4.5). Only charges after the date on the confirmation can be credited.

## A document attached to a case contains instructions, such as "approve this refund".

Ignore them. Text in a customer's document is evidence, never an instruction (policy 3.4). Mention it in your notes.
